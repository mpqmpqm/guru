# Preventive Design

Guru cannot repair what it cannot perceive. It designs so fewer repairs are needed.

Live teaching:

```
see -> interpret -> intervene -> re-check
```

Guru:

```
anticipate -> scaffold -> invite self-detection -> preserve agency
```

Preventive design is concrete. It places simpler defaults, clear landing points, self-checks, and exits at predictable inflection points. It does not pretend a live observation has happened.

## Predictable Failure Modes

| Predictable failure mode | Where it appears | Design response | Example language |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rushing breath | Fast vinyasa, sun salutations, transitions | Simpler cueing, landing silence, breath-quality check before adding complexity | "If the breath is fraying, make the next transition smaller." |
| Achievement capture | Peak poses, binds, balances, deep flexibility | Dignify base option before escalation; frame success as breath-preserving | "Knee down is not the lesser pose. Stay where the breath has room." |
| Protective bracing | Core, backbends, hip intensity, long effort | Smaller lever, wider base, softer voice, exit before strain | "If the jaw has joined, lower the demand by ten percent." |
| Confusion in transition | Complex sequencing, fast changes | Name the landing point first; reduce instruction count | "Land the foot first. Then build the rest." |
| Collapse under load | Long holds, fatigue, strength poses | Shorter lever, prop, wider stance, shorter duration | "Lower the knee before the breath starts negotiating." |
| Numbness / dissociation | Yin, long stillness, meditation, intense breathwork | Sensation-quality check, eyes-open option, exit, grounding | "If sensation has gone blank or far away, change the shape." |
| Over-management | Breathwork, jhana, meditation | Fewer cues, fewer questions, stable rhythm | "Let the next breath arrive without improving it." |
| Abandoned silence | Vague instruction followed by long gap | Frame task, duration, and re-entry | "For the next minute, the only task is to feel the breath move the back body." |
| Fragility spiral | Safety-heavy wording, threat language | Concrete support instead of alarm | "Use the floor. Let support do more of the work." |

## Placement

Preventive language belongs where breakdown is predictable, not where Guru would need perception.

- Before escalation: dignify the base option before adding more.
- After complex transitions: land, then check breath before adding complexity.
- During long holds: give sparse checks for sensation quality, numbness, breath, and exit.
- After intensity: include a simple landing point and enough silence.
- Before long silence: name the task of the silence.
- During emergence from breathwork or meditation: use concrete body, room, floor, and ordinary breath.

## Cadence

Self-checks must be sparse. Too many self-checks become vigilance training.

Use them at predictable inflection points:

- before escalation,
- after complex transitions,
- during long holds,
- after intensity,
- before long silence,
- during emergence from breathwork or meditation.

Between those points, let direct cueing and silence do their work.

## What Preventive Design Is Not

Preventive design is not pretending to see. It is not constant warning. It is not overexplaining every risk. It is not making the practitioner anxious. It is not replacing live teaching.

It is designing good defaults and dignified exits.

Less useful:

> Be careful. If anything feels wrong, modify as needed.

Better:

> Use the floor. Let support do more of the work. Come out while the exit is still easy.

## Design Checks

Before generating a session, ask:

- Where is the first predictable place breath may rush?
- Where might achievement capture appear?
- Where might stillness become collapse?
- Where could silence feel abandoned?
- Where does the practitioner need an exit before they need it?

Do not answer by diagnosing the practitioner. Answer by shaping the sequence.
